# r064 — lgbm_xmas0_tgd_r3_ratio

**Author:** researcher (session researcher-20260913-2)

**Hypothesis:** H045 — training-target transformation on top of r061: fit the level-normalised
target y / s, s = roll_mean_28 + 1 (as-of-origin), with sample weight s^2, and forecast s x
prediction. The weighted loss equals the raw squared error row for row, so the change isolates
the hypothesis class: leaves become multipliers of each series' recent level, which should
transfer seasonal and horizon shapes across series of very different volume.

**What changed:** `src/model.py`: new class `LGBMRatioTarget` (`lgbm_xmas0_tgd_r3_ratio`),
subclass of `LGBMTgdRoll3`, overriding `_fit_predict` only (target / weight / rescale). Features,
params, origins and postprocess identical to r061.

**Result vs current best (r061, 0.803303):** WRMSSE 0.813500 (spread 0.002021), paired gain
-0.010197. Per fold (r061 -> r064): 1 0.8236 -> 0.8368 (+0.0132), 2 0.8142 -> 0.8343 (+0.0202),

(worst fold 2 +0.020), 3 PASS. Detail in runs/detail/r064.json.

Multiplicative leaves are clearly worse. With weights s^2 spanning four orders of magnitude,
the effective sample is a few hundred high-volume series, so every split is fit on far fewer
effective rows (the tripled seed spread says the same), and a ratio to a 28-day mean amplifies
the noise of that mean at long horizons. The additive raw-unit model, which already sees
roll_mean_28 as a feature, pools better.

**Verdict:** discard. Change reverted.
