First runs under the v9 year-round fold layout (8 folds, quarterly; 2 spring folds mirror the
holdout). Bagged, m5_screen. **r134** recipe (new v9 baseline), **r135** hist3y (H166), **r136**
yoye_h3y (H167).

## The decomposition kills them
Per-fold gain vs recipe on the TWO SPRING folds (the holdout analogs):
- 2015-03-30: recipe 0.5763 -> hist3y 0.6463 (**-0.0700**) / yoye_h3y 0.6480 (-0.0717)
- 2016-03-28: recipe 0.6680 -> hist3y 0.7122 (**-0.0442**) / yoye_h3y 0.7143 (-0.0463)
Spring mean gain vs recipe: hist3y **-0.0571**, yoye_h3y **-0.0590**. hist3y wins summer/fall/
winter (+0.006..+0.037 each) and loses BIG on spring. cond2 no-fold-regress FAILS (fold 4 delta
+0.072 >> tol). Since the holdout is a spring window, 3yr history would very likely REGRESS the
yardstick despite its positive aggregate. **H166, H167 discarded.**

## Two conclusions
1. The recipe is NOT spring-blind — it is its STRONGEST on spring (0.576 is its best fold), and
   its holdout 0.626 sits in its spring-fold range. The "winter->spring gap" was an artifact of
   the retired winter-only proxy, not a model weakness. More history hurts spring because the
   seasonal peak drifts year to year (recency > volume for a drifting peak).
2. The v9 year-round proxy immediately caught an aggregate false-positive that the old proxy (and
   a naive aggregate-optimizing loop) would have promoted, WITHOUT spending a holdout shot. The
   self-improvement engine's honest-proxy + frozen-gate integrity, proven a second time (first:
   the recursive+direct ensemble, v8).

## Implication for top-50
The cheap history lever is dead. A top-50 push needs a change that improves the recipe's SPRING
performance specifically; next step is the error decomposition on r134's spring folds (levels,
horizons, series). recipe6_calendar_l2 stays champion; nothing to promote.
